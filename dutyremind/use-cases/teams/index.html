The Challenge

Spreadsheet Chaos During Exam Periods

At collegiate and university departments, the exam period is notoriously intense. A single academic department coordinator is often tasked with assigning 35–50 professors, adjunct lecturers, and teaching assistants across two weeks of midterms or finals, spanning morning (FN: 09:30–12:30) and afternoon (AN: 13:30–16:30) examination sessions.

Like most educational institutions, the department already ran entirely on Google Workspace (Gmail, Google Drive, Google Sheets, Google Calendar). But managing duties purely via spreadsheets caused persistent operational headaches:

• The "Version Drift" Problem

When emergency adjustments happened (e.g. a faculty member fell ill), updated spreadsheets were emailed out. Faculty routinely looked at outdated PDF exports or sheet printouts, missing duty changes.

• Manual Calendar Entry Friction

Expecting 40+ busy faculty members to manually find their name in a dense spreadsheet and book 6–8 individual calendar slots led to committing to other duties, forgotten duties and frantic morning phone calls.

• Heavy Enterprise Systems Weren't a Good Fit

Full-scale enterprise workforce scheduling platforms introduced excessive overhead: complex administrative setups, per-seat licensing across large adjunct rosters, and requiring professors to log into an unfamiliar external portal, and learning a complex tool.

The Solution

Automating Invigilation Invites from the Existing Google Sheet

Rather than reinventing their workflow or onboarding staff to a new platform, the department coordinator installed DutyRemind directly from the Google Workspace Marketplace. Because DutyRemind runs inside Google Sheets as a native add-on, the coordinator continued using the exact same spreadsheet layout they had used for years.

1

Configure Forenoon & Afternoon Shifts

In DutyRemind's sidebar, the coordinator defined FN (09:15 to 12:30) and AN (13:15 to 16:30). Overrides were added for special shorter Friday exam sessions with a single click.

2

Mark Assignments with Simple 'X' Markers

The spreadsheet list maintained professors' university email addresses in the left column. For each exam date column, placing an X indicated which professor was assigned to that hall slot.

3

One-Click Calendar Synchronization

Clicking Extensions → DutyRemind → Sync Calendars generated clean calendar events directly on each faculty member's Google Calendar—including event reminders the day before and 30 minutes before the bell.

Key Takeaways

The Impact on Department Operations

Zero Missed Duties

Because events were placed directly into professors' native Google Calendars on their smartphones with 30-minute popups, forgotten invigilations dropped from multiple incidents per cycle to zero.

Zero Onboarding Friction

Faculty members did not have to download an app, create a new username, or remember another password. They just opened their usual Google Calendar widget or mobile notification.

100% Data Privacy & Ownership

University IT approved the setup immediately because all scheduling calculations run in google's servers, with strong emphasis on privacy by dutyRemind. Data in the sheet can be exported and stored locally for compliance and audit purposes.

More Ways Academic Teams Use DutyRemind

While exam invigilation is the most common use case, universities and educational organizations also deploy DutyRemind for:

  • Laboratory Supervision Rosters: Assigning teaching assistants and lab instructors to multi-hour physics, chemistry, or computing lab blocks. The slots can be marked with the Lab Names instead of X.
  • Department Admissions & Counseling Desks: Rotating faculty advising hours during freshman orientation and admissions cycles.
  • Campus Facility Supervision: Coordinating student worker hours across campus libraries, makerspaces, and recreation centers.
